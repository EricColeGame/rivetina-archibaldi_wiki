## Reading the Male Key: Practical Identification Tips

The most useful element of the **Rivetina archibaldi research findings** for field workers is the identification key, and the key is explicitly built around males. That single detail changes your workflow. If you cannot see male characters — and in *Rivetina* those characters are often subtle, involving proportions and abdominal structures — you should record your observation as *Rivetina* sp. rather than forcing a species name.

This is where a lot of community reports go wrong. Photographs that show a mantis from directly above, at low resolution, rarely carry enough information to run a key to species. Good records show the head from the front, the pronotum in profile, the wings at rest, and the underside of the abdomen where possible.

| Character to record | How to capture it | Why the key needs it |
|---|---|---|
| Head shape and eyes | Straight-on frontal shot | Separates genera and some species groups |
| Pronotum profile | Side view, whole body in frame | Proportions are diagnostic in *Rivetina* |
| Wing length vs. abdomen | Lateral shot at rest | Distinguishes adult males from nymphs |
| Abdominal tip | Underside or rear view | Male structures carry much of the diagnosis |
| Scale reference | Coin, ruler, or graph paper | Absolute size supports relative measurements |
| Locality and date | GPS plus habitat note | Ties your record to a documented population |

A second tip: photograph, do not collect. In many countries, including Iran, taking specimens requires permits, and a voucher you cannot legally keep is worse than a good photo series. Community reports on naturalist platforms are increasingly valuable precisely because they are non-lethal.
