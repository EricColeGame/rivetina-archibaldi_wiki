### Ambush, Not Pursuit

Ground mantises do not chase. They sit, wait, and let traffic come to them, then strike with the forelegs in a fraction of a second. A mantis built low and narrow is optimized for that tactic on open soil, where there is nowhere to hide except the litter itself.

### Generalist, Not Specialist

Nothing about the body plan suggests a narrow diet. Like most mantises, Rivetina archibaldi is best understood as an opportunistic carnivore that takes prey smaller than itself and ignores anything too large or too heavily armored to control. Cannibalism is also on the table — it is routine in mantis species kept at high density.

### Day-Active Ground Hunter

Mantises are vision-driven predators, and a ground-dwelling species hunting an open woodland floor is most likely active during daylight, when movement is easiest to detect. Night activity cannot be ruled out, which is exactly why the research framework further down suggests camera traps and night surveys alongside daytime observation.

## Habitat and Prey Availability in the Zagros Oak Woodlands

Rivetina archibaldi feeding habits are shaped first by geography. The species is associated with the oak woodland floor of Lorestan, Iran, and that habitat has a fairly predictable ground fauna: heavy ant traffic, a diverse beetle assemblage in the litter, wolf and ground spiders, seasonal bugs and juvenile grasshoppers, and flies and small moths resting near the soil.

| Prey group | Examples on the woodland floor | Likely importance | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ants | Ground-foraging Formicidae | **High** | Enormous numbers and constant movement make them the most encounter-prone prey |
| Beetles | Ground beetles, weevils, leaf beetles | **High** | Diverse, litter-dwelling, and the right size class for a slender mantis |
| Spiders | Wolf spiders, ground spiders | Medium | Fast and defensive, but manageable when small |
| True bugs and juvenile grasshoppers | Plant bugs, young Orthoptera | Medium, seasonal | Abundant in spring and early summer |
| Flies and small moths | Ground-resting adults | Lower | Taken opportunistically rather than targeted |

Two filters matter more than raw abundance. The first is **size**: a mantis will pass on prey it cannot safely subdue. The second is **encounter rate**: a slow-moving weevil two centimeters away is worth more than a fast spider across the clearing. This is why ants and beetles top the list even though they are not the largest available meals.

Community reports from mantis keepers follow the same pattern — ground-dwelling species generally accept small roaches, flies, and beetles in captivity, and often refuse hard-shelled adults until they are softened or pre-killed. That is keeper experience, not field data, but it lines up with what the morphology predicts.

No direct feeding study exists yet. As a ground-dwelling mantis, it is almost certainly a carnivorous ambush predator that takes small, ground-active arthropods — ants, beetles, and small spiders being the most likely staples in its Zagros oak woodland habitat in Lorestan, Iran.

**Q: Are Rivetina archibaldi feeding habits officially documented anywhere?**

**Q: Does it hunt like other praying mantises?**

Almost certainly yes. It has the raptorial forelegs, mobile triangular head, and predatory mouthparts found across Mantodea, which points to a classic sit-and-wait ambush: stay motionless, detect movement, and seize prey with the forelegs.
