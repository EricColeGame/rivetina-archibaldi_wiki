## How to Read the Paper: A Field Checklist

Taxonomic papers look dense, but they follow a predictable rhythm. Once you know which sections carry which information, a 12-page description reads like a well-organized dossier.

| Section | The question it answers | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | What is new here? | The one-sentence diagnosis and the type locality |
| Introduction | Why describe it now? | Regional context and prior *Rivetina* records for Iran |
| Materials and methods | How was the work done? | Specimen sources, depositories, imaging and measurement methods |
| Taxonomy | What exactly is this animal? | Holotype designation, differential diagnosis, description, etymology |
| Key | How do I identify it? | Couplet structure and which characters are decisive |
| Figures | What does it look like? | Habitus views, head and pronotum details, male genitalia where relevant |
| References | Who came before? | Foundational revisions and regional faunal works |

Three terms unlock most of the text:

1. **Holotype** — the single reference specimen that permanently anchors the name. Its depository is stated in the paper.
2. **Type locality** — the precise place the holotype came from. For this species, that is within the Zagros oak forests of Iran.
3. **Differential diagnosis** — the paragraph explaining exactly how the new species differs from its closest relatives. If you read only one part of a description, read this.

Pay attention to sex-specific language as well. The Iranian key is explicitly built on **males**, which is common in mantis taxonomy because male genitalia and antennae often provide the most reliable separating characters. If you are trying to identify a female, expect the key to be less decisive and plan to cross-check figures and measurements.
