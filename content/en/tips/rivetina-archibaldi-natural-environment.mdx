## Reading the Zagros Oak Forest Like a Mantis Hunter

The Zagros range runs along western Iran, and its oak woodlands are dominated by Persian oak (*Quercus brantii*). These are not dense, dark forests. They are open, park-like stands on rocky slopes and thin soils, with strong seasonality: wet, green springs; long, hot, dry summers; cold winters that can bring snow at higher elevations.

For a ground-dwelling mantis, that structure is everything. Open canopy means sunlight reaches the soil, which means basking spots. Thin soils and rock outcrops mean warm surfaces that hold heat into the evening. A sparse understory of grasses and low shrubs means short distances between perches — ideal for an ambush predator that would rather sprint than fly.

One caution: heavy livestock grazing compresses soil and strips the litter layer. Community reports from insect photographers working elsewhere in western Asia repeatedly describe *Rivetina* species as ground-huggers that run rather than take flight — a useful expectation, though it has not yet been formally documented for this species.

## Field Tips for Observing Rivetina archibaldi in Its Natural Environment

These are practical, low-impact techniques for naturalists, macro photographers, and students. None of them require collecting anything.

- **Walk slowly and scan close.** Ground mantises hold tight until you are almost on top of them, then bolt. A slow, three-meter-ahead scan beats a fast walk every time.
- **Get low.** Kneel, sit, or use a ground pod. Almost all the diagnostic detail on a *Rivetina* is on the animal's dorsal surface and forelegs, and you will not see it standing up.
- **Work the edges.** Track margins, gully lips, and the boundary between grazed and ungrazed ground concentrate both mantises and their prey.
- **Watch for movement, not shape.** Camouflage works. A sudden sprint across bare soil is usually your first and only cue.
- **Photograph the forelegs and the underside.** Species-level identification in this genus leans heavily on male morphology; a clear record of the specimen is worth more than a pretty portrait.
- **Check your permits.** Collecting insects in Iran requires authorization. Photography and observation generally do not, but confirm locally before you travel.

|---|---|---|
| Timing | Arrive at first light in late spring | Mantises bask on warm surfaces to start the day |
