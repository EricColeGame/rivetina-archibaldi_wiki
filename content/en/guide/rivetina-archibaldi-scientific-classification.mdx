### Order Mantodea

Mantodea is the order that separates mantises from crickets, grasshoppers, and cockroaches — groups they are sometimes confused with. Mantises are defined by raptorial forelegs built for grabbing prey, a highly mobile triangular head, and a predatory life cycle from the first instar onward. Placing a new insect in Mantodea is usually the easiest step in the process; the hard work happens further down.

### Genus *Rivetina*

## What "sp. nov." Actually Means

The phrase *species nova*, abbreviated *sp. nov.*, is the formal flag that a paper is naming something previously unknown to science. It is not a synonym for "newly discovered in the wild" — a specimen can sit in a museum drawer for decades before someone recognizes it as undescribed.

| Abbreviation | Meaning | Why it matters to readers |
|---|---|---|
| *sp. nov.* | New species being described | Signals the formal naming act |
| *sp.* | An undetermined species of a genus | The exact species is unknown |
| *cf.* | Compares with, but not confirmed | The identification is provisional |
| *aff.* | Related to, but likely distinct | Suggests a possible undescribed relative |
| *syn.* | Synonym | An older name now folded into another |

## Common Misconceptions About Mantis Taxonomy

- **"A new species means a new discovery in the field."** Often it means a new interpretation of specimens already in collections.
- **"The classification is permanent."** It is a working hypothesis. Genera get revised and families get reorganized as molecular data accumulates.
- **"One photo is enough to identify a *Rivetina*."** In most cases it is not; diagnostic characters often sit on the forelegs and head, which photographs frequently miss.
- **"Higher ranks are the important ones."** For practical work, genus and species do almost all the heavy lifting.
